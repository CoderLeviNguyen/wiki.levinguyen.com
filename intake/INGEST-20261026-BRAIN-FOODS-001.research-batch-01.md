## Verified bibliographic sources (metadata and abstract read)

### SRC-CANDIDATE-01 — Systematic review
- Title: *Nut Consumption for Cognitive Performance: A Systematic Review*
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/advances/nmaa153
- PMID: 33330927
- Year: 2021
- Retrieval: Europe PMC search, `EXT_ID:33330927 AND SRC:MED`, `resultType=core`, accessed 2026-10-26
- Design: systematic review of 22 studies (43,793 participants), searches through April 2020
- Abstract finding: mixed-nut evidence on cognitive outcomes was inconsistent; of six walnut studies, including two randomized controlled trials, only one did not show a positive association.
- Relevance: BF-01 walnuts/memory, BF-02 walnuts/cognitive function; context for other nuts but **not direct proof of each species-specific benefit**.
- Limitation: abstract only; heterogeneity; association is not automatically causal; populations and doses vary.
- Assessment: **POSSIBLE_SUPPORT, NOT VERIFIED**.

### SRC-CANDIDATE-02 — Almond randomized crossover trial
- Title: *Long-term effects of almond consumption on cognitive performance and brain vascular function: a randomized crossover trial in overweight/obese adults with prediabetes*
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41430-026-01705-6
- PMID: 41691115
- Year: 2026
- Retrieval: Europe PMC search, `EXT_ID:41691115 AND SRC:MED`, `resultType=core`, accessed 2026-10-26
- Design: randomized, single-blind controlled crossover trial; 50 g almonds/day for 5 months vs control; 43 enrolled, 34 completed.
- Abstract finding: no statistically significant intervention effect on measured cognitive performance outcomes or cerebral blood flow.
- Relevance: BF-07 almonds/memory; BF-08 almonds/learning only indirectly, as learning was not explicitly established as a measured endpoint in the retrieved abstract.
- Limitation: specific prediabetes population, small completion sample, abstract only; null finding does not prove impossibility of benefits in other groups.
- Assessment: **EVIDENCE_AGAINST_GENERALIZED_COGNITIVE_CLAIM, NOT VERIFIED**.
