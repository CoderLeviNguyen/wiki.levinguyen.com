Status: RESEARCHED_PARTIALLY; all 14 claims PENDING and PRIVATE. No independent reviewer decision; no VERIFIED claim.

## Source ledger (URLs opened or search results read)
1. Systematic review of nuts/cognition (2021), PMID 33330927, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8166568/ — 22 studies, heterogeneous results; walnut findings favorable in some studies but no definitive generalized effect.
2. Walnut systematic review/meta-analysis (2021), PMID 34132152, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34132152/ — five cognition RCTs did not show significant *overall* cognitive effects; some subgroup/domain effects. Evidence not conclusive.
3. Almond randomized crossover (2026), PMID 41691115, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41691115/ — 50 g/day, five months, prediabetes; no significant cognitive improvement in tested sample.
4. Almond open-label RCT (2026), PMID 41850683, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41850683/ — 60 middle-aged Indian adults with prediabetes, 24 weeks; some executive-function/processing-speed benefits; supported by Almond Board of California. This contrasts with source 3; not proof of improved memory or learning across populations.
5. Raisin RCT (2023), PMID 37375715, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37375715/ — 80 older adults, 50 g/day for six months; some cognitive measures improved, not a direct trial of calmness or necessarily concentration.
6. Pistachio pilot (2024), PMID 38943918, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38943918/ — overweight young adults; selective/sustained attention measures improved; pilot results need confirmation and population limits.
7. Pistachio RCT *protocol* (2026), PMID 41991057, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41991057/ — protocol only, NOT outcome evidence.
8. Cashew maternal animal study (2017), PMID 28663041, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28663041/ — offspring reflex/memory effects in animals, cannot extrapolate to human brain development.
9. Dried apricot extract laboratory study (2025), PMID 40678329, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40678329/ — antioxidant assays of extract, not proof that consuming dried apricots prevents human brain oxidative stress.
10. Mixed nuts crossover RCT (2023), PMID 37296019, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37296019/ — intervention includes several nuts; cannot attribute findings to cashews or pistachios alone.

## Evidence rules and remaining tasks
- Search findings are NOT proof that no other studies exist.
- Do not confuse improved cognition with concentration, learning, calmness, alertness or fatigue without matching measured endpoints.
- Never extrapolate animal or in-vitro results to human clinical benefits.
- Record conflicting findings, study populations, dosage, duration, control, endpoints and funding.
- Before VERIFIED: retrieve and evaluate full texts where possible; confirm all metadata, quality and limitations; obtain genuine independent reviewer decision.
